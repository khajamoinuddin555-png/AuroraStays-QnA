## 1. Purpose and guest standard

AuroraStay wants a safe, clean, and welcoming stay for guests, visitors, and staff. These guidelines apply in guest rooms, public areas, parking areas, meeting spaces, and property-operated transportation. They supplement the reservation terms, posted notices, emergency instructions, and applicable law.

Staff should address conduct concerns early, use neutral language, and apply rules consistently. A staff member should not confront a guest alone when there is a safety risk. Contact the manager on duty or emergency services according to the severity of the situation.

## Immediate safety rule

Call 911 for an immediate threat to life, serious injury, fire, active violence, or suspected crime in progress. Notify the manager on duty when safe. Do not delay emergency care to complete a complaint record.

## 3. Accessibility and service animals

AuroraStay follows the Americans with Disabilities Act Title III requirements for places of lodging and provides reasonable modifications and accessible features as required by applicable law. Staff should focus on the requested feature or task, not on the guest's diagnosis or private medical information.

| Question or issue                                     | Required handling                                                                                                                                             |
|-------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| A guest requests an accessible feature                | Ask what feature is needed, check the room and property profile, record the request, and confirm what can be provided. Escalate if the request cannot be met. |
| A service animal is not obvious                       | Ask only whether the animal is required because of a disability and what work or task it has been trained to perform.                                         |
| A service animal is out of control or not housebroken | Ask the handler to correct the issue. If it is not corrected, involve the manager. Do not charge a pet fee or make a breed-based decision.                    |
| A guest asks about an emotional support animal        | An animal that does not meet the ADA service animal definition follows the property's pet policy unless another law or approved accommodation applies.        |
| A guest reports discrimination                        | Listen, do not argue, document the facts, involve the manager, and offer the formal complaint channel. Escalate urgent safety or access concerns immediately. |

Staff must not demand a service animal certificate, vest, or medical record as a condition of entry. Service animals are not subject to a pet fee. A direct threat assessment must be based on objective evidence and an individualized assessment, not on stereotypes.

## 4. Safety, privacy, and lost property

Guests should use the room safe when available and keep keys, mobile credentials, and personal information secure. Staff verify identity before issuing a replacement key, providing room information, or releasing stored luggage. Staff must not announce a guest's room number aloud in public.

Lost property is logged, secured, and returned through the approved process. Staff should not open, browse, or share personal content from a found device except as needed to identify the owner through an approved procedure. Suspected weapons, drugs, exploitation, or immediate danger are handled through safety escalation, not informal investigation.

## 5. Complaint resolution

| Stage                    | Staff action                                                                                                                           | Target                                |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Listen and stabilize     | Move to a safe and private setting where possible. Confirm the immediate need and acknowledge the concern without assigning blame.     | Immediately                           |
| Clarify facts            | Ask what happened, when, where, who was involved, and what outcome the guest needs. Avoid leading questions.                           | During first contact                  |
| Check policy and records | Review reservation, property profile, posted rule, folio, or incident record. Protect sensitive data.                                  | Same shift                            |
| Offer a remedy           | Give the remedy the staff member can approve, or explain the manager review and follow-up time.                                        | Same shift when possible              |
| Escalate and close       | Escalate safety, access, discrimination, privacy, media, legal, or unresolved compensation issues. Confirm the next step to the guest. | Within one business day unless urgent |

## Neutral documentation

Record observable facts, the guest's stated concern, the policy checked, the action taken, the approver, and the follow-up owner. Avoid labels such as difficult, intoxicated, or suspicious unless the specific observable behavior is recorded and the label is necessary for safety.

## 8. Review notes

This policy should be reviewed at least annually and after a material incident, regulatory change, property acquisition, or change to payment, access, surveillance, or guest messaging systems. Regulatory references to confirm include ADA Title III, applicable state and local lodging rules, privacy and breach notification requirements, OSHA workplace safety requirements, and PCI DSS controls for payment data.

Nothing in this guide authorizes staff to search a guest, confiscate property, promise confidentiality beyond company policy, or take action that conflicts with local law or an emergency responder's direction.
